## What is a Guarantee of Origin?

A Guarantee of Origin (GO) is an electronic certificate that proves a specific quantity of energy was produced from a particular source. In Europe, one GO is issued for every megawatt-hour (MWh) of energy produced.

When a company claims it consumed renewable electricity, it is typically backed by GOs. When an energy supplier markets a "green" electricity tariff, GOs are the underlying evidence.

## How the system works

The European GO system is coordinated by the **Association of Issuing Bodies (AIB)** under a framework called the European Energy Certificate System (EECS). Each EU Member State has an authorised issuing body — in France, for example, this is EEX (European Energy Exchange).

The lifecycle of a GO has four stages:

1. **Issuance** — when a renewable energy producer generates electricity, the national issuing body creates GOs based on metered production data, typically reported monthly.
2. **Transfer** — GOs can be bought and sold between account holders through national registries, and transferred across borders via the AIB hub.
3. **Cancellation** — when a company wants to claim renewable energy consumption, it cancels the relevant GOs, specifying the beneficiary and consumption period.
4. **Expiry** — GOs expire 12 months after the end of the production period if not cancelled.

## What the system does well

The GO system provides a standardised, pan-European mechanism for tracking renewable energy origin. It is legally recognised under EU directives, widely adopted, and supported by a mature registry infrastructure.

For annual renewable energy reporting — such as corporate sustainability disclosures or national renewable energy targets — it serves its purpose.

## What the system cannot currently prove

The GO system was designed around what was technically feasible in the early 2000s. Several limitations are increasingly relevant as energy markets evolve:

**Temporal mismatch** — GOs are issued monthly. A company can buy GOs from Norwegian hydro produced in January and use them to claim green consumption in December. The certificate and the actual consumption are decoupled in time.

**Location mismatch** — GOs can be traded across borders. A company consuming electricity from a coal-heavy grid can purchase GOs from a distant renewable source. The physical reality and the paper claim are geographically disconnected.

**Administrative trust** — the system relies on trust in issuing bodies, registries, and metering infrastructure. There is no cryptographic proof that the underlying data is accurate.

## What comes next

The energy industry is moving toward **24/7 carbon-free energy matching** — the idea that renewable consumption should be matched to renewable production hour by hour, in the same location. Companies like Google and Microsoft have committed to this standard.

Meeting this standard requires more granular data, more reliable verification, and — for organisations that handle commercially sensitive operational data — the ability to prove claims without revealing everything.

That is where new verification technology becomes relevant.
